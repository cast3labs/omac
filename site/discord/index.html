Omac 1.0 · beta · Terms

Why does Omac need Accessibility? Is it safe?

macOS lets an app move other apps' windows only through Accessibility, so every window manager asks for it, once. Omac is signed and notarized by Apple, which macOS checks before it opens, and the app runs nothing as root. More on what it needs.

Does it send anything?

Not unless you tick a box. The first time it opens, Omac asks two questions, both unticked: a daily count with no identifier, and update checks. Your windows, settings and clipboard history stay on your Mac. Every connection Omac can make.

How does it update?

Choose Check for Updates… in Omac's menu, or say yes to a daily check the first time it opens. An update installs only when you choose Install, and only if it carries Omac's signature.

How do I become a founder, and what if I'm late?

Open Omac for the first time before 1 February 2027, or before 1,000 founders have joined. There is no sign-up: Omac notes the day it first opens on your Mac, and nothing is sent. After that, Omac will cost US$12 once, with a 30-day trial, and nothing will lock. In return, tell us what breaks: omac@ghostype.ca or an issue.

Does it run on Intel Macs?

No. Omac needs Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later.

Do my usual shortcuts still work? Do I need Omarchy?

⌘C, ⌘V and ⌘Tab work with either ⌘. Where Omac uses the left ⌘, the right ⌘ still does what your apps expect, such as right ⌘ Shift 4 for a screenshot. You don't need Omarchy: Omac borrows its keybindings, and it is not made by or affiliated with Omarchy.

How do I pause or remove it?

Left ⌘Ctrl⌥Esc pauses the tiling; press it again to resume. Quitting Omac puts every window back on screen. To remove it, follow Uninstall.
