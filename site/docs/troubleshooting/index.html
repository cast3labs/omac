Troubleshooting

You see What to do
Left ⌘ shortcuts do nothing Omac needs the Accessibility permission (how). omac status says accessibility false until it has it.
omac: command not found Open a new Terminal window. The installer set this up for new windows only.
"Omac is an app downloaded from the Internet…" macOS asks this once, the first time you open a copy downloaded with a web browser. Click Open.
Omac was not installed: … The message says why. Run the command again; if it happens again, send the full output.
A window is off screen omac rescue brings it back.

Support is best effort: one person maintains Omac. To report a bug, open an issue with the output of omac status and what you pressed. Report a security problem privately, through GitHub's private vulnerability report form, never in an issue.

Two copies of Omac

Only one Omac may run. From 1.0.0, when a second copy opens, one of the two says so and asks which one to keep: the copy that opens, or, when a copy of an earlier preview opens after it, the copy that was already running (a copy that quits does so the normal way, putting back any window it had hidden). The suggested answer keeps the copy in your Applications folder over one opened where it was downloaded, then the newer version. When the downloaded copy is the newer version, the alert also says how to use it: quit Omac, move the downloaded copy to your Applications folder in place of the old one, then open it. Two copies of an earlier preview do not ask, so quit an old one yourself. If shortcuts stop, or an update seems not to take, a second copy may be starting from another folder: the one-line installer keeps Omac in ~/Applications/Omac.app, and an older copy in /Applications conflicts with it. omac status lists every other copy that is running under running_copies, and every other copy installed on the Mac under other_copies. Quit Omac (omac quit), delete the copy you do not want, and open the one you keep. Running the install command again removes the /Applications copy for you (it may ask for your Mac password to do so).

Pausing Omac

Left ⌘ Ctrl ⌥ Esc pauses the tiling: windows stop moving, and the window keys (focus, move, resize, split, float) pass through to the app in front. Launch keys, workspace keys and the tools (palette, clipboard history, theme menu) keep working. Press it again to resume. While paused, the menu-bar icon shows ⏸.

Shortcuts stop while a terminal or a password field is open

While a password field has the keyboard, or a terminal has Secure Keyboard Entry turned on (in Terminal's or iTerm2's app menu), macOS keeps the keys you type from every other app, Omac's shortcuts included. They work again as soon as it ends. Meanwhile omac status shows secure_input true and says what to do, and Omac's log notes when it starts and ends. Omac names the app that holds it when macOS reports it: omac status shows it under secure_input_app (for example Terminal, pid 812), and the menu's hotkey row and the log line name it too. Leave the password field, or turn Secure Keyboard Entry off in the terminal's app menu; if it stays on, quit the app where you last typed a password (some apps leave it on until they quit).

The keyboard feels slow

Omac asks each app where its windows are every 2 seconds, and a question to an app that stops answering (stopped at a debugger breakpoint, or showing the spinning wait cursor) would make every key on the Mac wait. So Omac leaves such an app out of those checks and asks again after 5 seconds, then after longer waits, up to 30 seconds, while it still does not answer; the first answer ends the wait. omac status lists those apps by process number under quarantined_pids (ps -p <number> names one), and ax_timeouts counts the requests that got no answer since Omac started. When a request to move or focus one of an app's windows gets no answer, Omac also stops moving that app's windows for 5 seconds, then tries again: blocked_pids lists those apps. An app that answers, but slowly, is never left out, and your keys wait while Omac waits for its answer. Each 2-second check asks it one question, then leaves it until the next check: while nothing changes, a check holds your keys for one of its answers at most, plus at most about an eighth of a second for the other apps (a check usually holds your keys for at most about 0.33 s beside an app that takes 0.2 s to answer; 0.23 s in Omac's simulation of a typical desk; an app that is usually quick but stalls only now and then can add one of its stalls, once; an app slower than 0.25 s counts as not answering). A window of it that moved costs a question or two more, and a new window of it that appeared without notice costs nine, once. Beside it, the apps that answer at once are still checked every few seconds on a desk of a handful of apps, less often on a crowded one. omac stats counts the slow answers under slow reads. tap_reenabled above 0 means macOS switched Omac's shortcuts off for a moment because Omac answered too slowly, and Omac switched them back on. tap is true only while macOS has them switched on; Omac checks every 5 seconds and switches them back on if they are off.

A window you cannot reach

omac rescue moves any window that is off every display back onto one. Reorganize in Omac's menu (or omac reorganize) re-tiles every workspace: it evens out the split sizes and resumes tiling if it was paused. Quitting Omac (omac quit) puts every hidden window back on screen.

After a macOS update

If shortcuts do nothing after a macOS update, check the Accessibility permission first: open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, turn Omac off and on again, then check omac status.
