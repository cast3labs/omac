Omac privacy policy

Version: 3, 2026-10-09; approved by the maintainer on 2026-10-09. The versions and drafts before it are listed under "Changes to this policy". Applies to: the Omac app and its command-line tool omac, Omac's own server t.ghostype.ca, and the website omac.ghostype.ca. Version française : PRIVACY.fr.md. Both say the same thing.

The short version

  • Omac sends nothing unless you ask it to. Only two features can send something on their own, and both stay off until you tick their box: a daily usage count and automatic update checks.
  • The daily usage count is one small message a day: the Omac version, the macOS version, the number of displays and two yes/no flags. It carries no identifier. Omac's server never reads or stores your IP address. Cloudflare, which runs that server, sees the address in order to deliver the message; what Cloudflare keeps is described below.
  • An update check asks GitHub, which hosts Omac's website and downloads, whether a newer version exists.
  • Everything else stays on your Mac: your settings, your window layout, your clipboard history and the files Omac needs to run.
  • Omac sells no data, shows no ads and builds no profiles.
  • The earlier previews of Omac never send the daily count and never check for updates. The first version that can is 1.0.0.
  • Purchases and licences are not covered yet. Nothing is sold yet, and no version of Omac collects purchase or licence data.
  • Questions: omac@ghostype.ca.

Who is responsible

Omac is made by cast3labs, in Québec, Canada. cast3labs decides what Omac collects and why (the "controller", in the words of Europe's GDPR). The person in charge of the protection of personal information for Omac under Québec's private-sector privacy law (Law 25, section 3.1) is the maintainer, the person who runs cast3labs, reachable at omac@ghostype.ca.

Contact: omac@ghostype.ca. Write to this address about anything in this policy, including for help understanding what a checkbox means before you tick it. Mail to this address passes through Cloudflare Email Routing, which forwards it to the maintainer's own mailbox. That mailbox is a Gmail account, run by Google LLC in the United States. Cloudflare keeps a log of each message it forwards (its sender, its recipient, when it came and whether it was delivered), which the maintainer can see for up to 30 days.

The daily usage count (optional)

Off unless you tick its box. Omac asks once, in its first window, in a box of its own, apart from the update question. Leaving the box unticked answers no, and Omac does not ask again. If you quit Omac while that window is open, or Omac cannot save your answer in omac.toml, nothing is recorded or sent, and the window asks again at a later launch, once Omac can save the answer. Omac writes each answer with consent_version, the version of the box's text you answered. A yes without it, or with an older number, counts as no answer: that covers a yes given in an earlier preview of Omac, whose menu row said the count was not active yet, and a line typed by hand. Nothing is sent for it, and the window asks again, with the box unticked. You can change your answer at any time in the Omac menu ("Share anonymous daily count") or in your settings file, ~/.config/omac/omac.toml:

[privacy]
daily_ping = true    # false, or no line at all, sends nothing
consent_version = 2  # written by Omac with your answer; a true without it sends nothing

What it is for: counting how many Macs run Omac, on which versions of Omac and macOS, and with how many displays, so the maintainer knows what to support. It is used for nothing else.

What is sent

At most one successful HTTPS message a day (see "When"), exactly like this example. The values change; the keys never do.

{"displays":"2","first_ever":false,"first_this_month":false,"os":"26.1","schema":1,"version":"1.0.0"}
Key What it is Possible values
schema the format of the message 1
version the Omac version a version such as 1.0.0; anything else is sent as unknown
os the macOS version, first two numbers only such as 26.1; the third number is never sent
displays how many displays are connected 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5+
first_this_month this is the Mac's first message this calendar month (UTC) true or false
first_ever this is the first message since the count was turned on true or false

It never contains an identifier, your key presses, window titles, app names, file names or contents, your name, your Apple ID, your language or your location. The messages share no common key, so the server cannot tell which ones came from the same Mac. One limit: an unusual setup (a rare mix of versions and display count) can make one Mac's messages stand out from the others for as long as the raw messages are kept (see "What is kept").

If you turn the count off and on again, your next message says first_ever: true and first_this_month: true again, so the public numbers can count the same install more than once.

The request also carries fixed headers that say nothing about you: User-Agent: Omac/<version> (the same version as in the message), Content-Type: application/json and Accept-Language: en (fixed, so your list of languages is not sent). macOS adds the standard Host, Content-Length, Connection, Accept and Accept-Encoding headers. The request carries no cookie, and nothing about it is cached.

When

  • At most one successful message per day, counted in UTC.
  • The first message after you turn the count on goes on the next UTC day, never the day you turn it on.
  • Never sooner than a random 5 to 60 minutes after Omac starts or your Mac wakes, so the time of a message tells when you sat down only to within an hour.
  • A message the server refuses is tried again later: after 10 minutes, then twice as long each time, up to 6 hours apart. When the server asks Omac to wait longer (an HTTP 429 answer with a Retry-After header in seconds), Omac waits as long as it asks, up to 24 hours. A Mac that was off for a week sends one message when it is back, not seven.
  • A message that gets no answer from the server, for example because your Mac is offline or the connection drops, counts as that day's message, since it may have arrived: the next one goes on the next UTC day.
  • Only a release build of Omac, signed by the maintainer, can send it. A copy built from source never does.
  • Right before each send, Omac reads omac.toml again, and sends only if it still says daily_ping = true.

Who receives it, and what they see

The message goes to https://t.ghostype.ca/v1/ping: a small server (a Cloudflare Worker with a Cloudflare D1 database) that Cloudflare runs for the maintainer.

  • Your IP address reaches Cloudflare with every message, as it does with any connection. Cloudflare also sees the headers above and the technical details of the encrypted connection.
  • Omac's server code never reads your IP address, any header, or the location that Cloudflare attaches to each request, and it writes no log. The server's request logs (Workers Logs) and their export (Logpush) are switched off. A test on 2026-09-27 showed that, with those logs off, the Worker's logs keep no record of a request.
  • What Cloudflare does itself is outside Omac's control. Cloudflare's security tools show the account owner samples of recent requests, and of the requests its rules blocked or limited, with their IP addresses, for up to 31 days on the free plan Omac uses. A rate-limiting rule that protects the server counts requests per IP address. A second rule refuses a message whose User-Agent does not name Omac. Cloudflare's live log tool can show requests as they arrive; the maintainer does not leave it running and never saves what it shows. Cloudflare describes its own handling of IP addresses in its privacy policy: https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/.

What the public statistics show

The totals are public at https://t.ghostype.ca/stats and https://t.ghostype.ca/stats.json: opted-in installs per day and, for each calendar month once it is over, per month; for the latest such month, its Omac versions, macOS versions and display counts, counting each install once, though an install that turned the count off and on that month can be counted more than once; and download counts. Any number of installs per day or per month under 5 is shown as <5, zero included. In the tables, a version, macOS version or display count that fewer than 5 installs share is counted in "other", and "other" is never under 5: while it would be, even at 0, it takes the smallest named value too, so every number in a table is 5 or more. A month is shown only once it is over, and its numbers never change after that. Download counts, which count files and not people, are shown even when under 5. Nothing on the page describes a person.

Opening the page shows Cloudflare your IP address and what your browser sends with any request, such as its name and your preferred languages (see "Who receives it, and what they see" for what Cloudflare keeps); Omac's server code reads none of it, and the page sets no cookie and loads nothing from anyone else.

Anyone can send a message that looks like Omac's, and the server cannot tell it from a real one, so the counts can include fakes. Whoever sends fakes knows how many they sent, so to them a number under 5 is not hidden: they could learn how many installs sent a count on a given day, whether one install with an uncommon setup was active in a given month, and even whether its first count that month came on a day they pick; never which install, and nothing about it beyond the values its messages carry and the days they arrived.

How to turn it off

Any one of these stops the count at once:

  • in the Omac menu, untick "Share anonymous daily count";
  • run omac telemetry off;
  • set daily_ping = false in the [privacy] table of ~/.config/omac/omac.toml.

Omac erases telemetry.json as soon as it reads an omac.toml that says no. If Omac cannot save omac.toml, it tells you, and the count starts again the next time Omac reads the file's yes; telemetry.json stays until then, so starting again does not send a second message the same day.

omac status shows your answer (telemetry: not-asked, on or off) and the day of the last message (telemetry_last_sent). Omac works the same whether the count is on or off.

Update checks (optional)

Off unless you tick its box, which is separate from the daily count's box. Change it at any time in the Omac menu, or with check_updates in the [privacy] table of omac.toml. As for the count, a yes without the consent_version Omac writes with each answer (a line typed by hand before version 1.0.0) counts as no answer: Omac does not check, and the window asks again.

What it is for: telling you when a newer version of Omac exists. Omac uses Sparkle, a widely used open-source updater for Mac apps.

What an update check sends

  • When: at most once a day, and only while check_updates = true. "Check for Updates…" in the Omac menu checks once, when you choose it, whether the box is ticked or not.
  • What: one request for the list of versions at https://omac.ghostype.ca/appcast.xml. Like any web request it carries your IP address, and it names the Omac and Sparkle versions. Its Accept-Language header is fixed to en, as the daily count's is, so your list of languages is not sent. It sends no description of your Mac (Sparkle's "system profile" is switched off) and no identifier.
  • Nothing is downloaded or installed until you choose to install an update. The update is then downloaded from GitHub, and Omac checks its signatures before installing it.
  • A copy built from source never checks, and neither does a copy running from a Nix or MacPorts folder: Nix or MacPorts updates it. Omac recognises such a copy by its folder, or for Nix by the date Nix gives its files; one it does not recognise is treated as a downloaded copy, and checks while the box is ticked. A downloaded copy that macOS runs from a temporary location, before you move it to your Applications folder, does not check until you move it. A copy installed with Homebrew checks, as a downloaded copy does, while the box is ticked. The folder makes no difference to the daily count: a copy sends it only if it is a release build and its own box is ticked.

Who receives it

GitHub, which hosts omac.ghostype.ca (on GitHub Pages) and Omac's downloads, in the United States. GitHub logs the IP address of every visit to a GitHub Pages site for security purposes, and counts how many times each release file is downloaded (the counts are public numbers). The maintainer gets no visitor logs from GitHub Pages, so he cannot see who checked. GitHub describes its own handling in its privacy statement: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement.

On your Mac, Sparkle keeps a few facts in Omac's macOS preferences: when it last checked, a version you chose to skip, that it has run before, where its update window was on the screen, and a random number, never sent, that decides when a version released gradually reaches your Mac.

Also good to know:

  • Key presses. To recognise its shortcuts, Omac watches the keyboard, which is why it needs the Accessibility permission. It does not record or send what you type. Its log names only an Omac shortcut when one fires.
  • Permissions. Besides Accessibility, Omac asks for the Microphone only for dictation (it listens only while dictation is on: while you hold the dictation keys, or from one press of the toggle key to the next, a minute at most unless you change that in omac.toml), for Screen Recording only to read text from the screen, for Automation to open a terminal (for a coding agent, or with "Finish in Terminal" when Homebrew could not complete an install) and to switch macOS between light and dark, and for Notifications for your reminders and for Omac's short notices (about text read from the screen, dictation, read aloud, themes, its first window and update checks).
  • Other apps. When you choose a theme, Omac writes colour files for apps you have installed (such as Ghostty, Claude Code, opencode, Sublime Text, VS Code and iTerm2), changes the wallpaper, and switches macOS between light and dark. It changes another app's own settings only after you run omac theme adopt <app>, except the colour setting of Chrome and Brave, which a theme change also updates when one is already set. omac theme off puts the wallpaper back and removes Omac's own theme folder (~/.local/state/omac/current/); it leaves the rest as it is:
  • macOS stays in light or dark mode (change it in System Settings, Appearance);
  • the colour files Omac wrote in other apps' folders stay, such as ~/.claude/themes/omac.json, iTerm2's DynamicProfiles/omac.json, Sublime Text's Omac.sublime-color-scheme and VS Code's ~/.vscode/extensions/omac-theme; delete them to remove them;
  • the Chrome and Brave colour setting stays; this command removes it for Chrome (for Brave, use com.brave.Browser): defaults delete com.google.Chrome BrowserThemeColor;
  • the settings of an app you adopted stay until you change them back in that app.
  • Launch at login is registered with macOS's login items. A first install with the one-line installer turns it on; an update leaves your choice as it is. omac login off, or "Launch at login" in the Omac menu, turns it off, and omac login on turns it back on.
  • The installer puts Omac in ~/Applications/Omac.app and the omac command in ~/.local/bin, may add two marked lines to your shell's startup file so the command is found, and on a first install turns on launch at login (above).

Your rights

You have these rights wherever you live. They come from Québec's Law 25, Canada's PIPEDA and, for people in the European Union or the European Economic Area, the GDPR.

  • Withdraw your consent at any time, as easily as you gave it: see "How to turn it off". It stops future messages at once.
  • Know what is collected and why: this policy, and omac status on your Mac.
  • Access, correction, deletion, restriction of processing and a copy in a common format. For the daily count, the messages carry no identifier, so the maintainer cannot tell which rows came from your Mac and cannot find, show, correct, restrict or delete them for you (GDPR Art. 11(2)). Raw rows disappear within about 7 days, plus up to 7 in the backup; the totals describe no one. If you give details that single out your rows, for example a rare setup, the maintainer will act on your request as far as those details allow. Everything else Omac keeps is on your Mac, where you can read or delete it. For mail you sent, write to omac@ghostype.ca.
  • Object to any use of your data, and complain (see "Complaints").

Answers are free, and come within 30 days.

No representative in the European Union. Omac has not appointed a representative in the EU under GDPR Art. 27. The maintainer's reasons are recorded with this policy's records.

Complaints

  1. Write to omac@ghostype.ca first. You get an answer within 30 days.
  2. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to:
  3. in Québec, the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec: https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/
  4. in Canada, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/
  5. in the European Union or the European Economic Area, your country's data protection authority: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en

Security

  • The daily count goes only over HTTPS, to one fixed address. Omac does not follow redirects for it, sends no cookie and keeps no copy of the request.
  • The server's database has no column for an IP address, a browser or app name, a location or an identifier, and its code never reads them. Its request logs are off.
  • telemetry.json and the clipboard history files are readable only by your user account on your Mac.
  • An update is installed only when its signatures match the maintainer's.

Children

Omac is not meant for children. In Québec, a person under 14 needs a parent's consent before turning on the daily count; in the EU, the age is between 13 and 16 depending on the country.

Governance

The maintainer is the only person with access to Omac's server, its records and the contact address. He:

  • approves this policy and every change to it;
  • keeps the data only as long as the table in "What is kept" says, and deletes it as that table describes;
  • answers requests and complaints within 30 days;
  • keeps a register of confidentiality incidents and, when an incident carries the risk each law names, reports it to the Commission d'accès à l'information, to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and, for people in the European Union or the European Economic Area, to their data protection authorities; he tells the people concerned directly when he can reach them, such as people who wrote to him, and by a public notice when he cannot, as with the daily count, whose senders he does not know;
  • reviews this policy, and the statistics kept with no end date, once a year and whenever what Omac sends changes.
